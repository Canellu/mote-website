Mode: Persuade. Audience: desktop users with Philips Hue who want lighting control inside their workflow; Windows is the initial release platform. Primary action: understand Mote and review its features before the Microsoft Store release.

Proof: rooms, zones, scenes, dashboard, widgets, and PC Sync shown through truthful interface demonstrations. No testimonials, metrics, download promise, or pricing.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The page moves through one continuous field of softly changing daylight, with real product color supplying contrast; it refuses the generic hero-and-feature-card stack and decorative control metaphors.

OWN-WORLD: Graphite and mineral-white fields, neon orange, solid control surfaces, technical ticks, minimal rounding, and precise Geist typography across display and interface roles.

STORY: See Mote working, inspect dashboard, appearance settings, and Sync Box setup, explore room controls, scene browsing, and light placement through one consolidated evidence gallery, then inspect three widget-creation states through a second selector before checking release status. A single light site presentation holds dark application captures in contrast.

FIRST VIEWPORT: A full-width sticky glass header fades naturally into the page above a centered headline and description. The top of a large, real Mote window enters halfway through the fold; scrolling enlarges it and resolves concise dashboard facts around it before the next chapter.

SIGNATURE INTERACTION: An explicitly illustrative Mote control lets visitors switch scenes, adjust brightness, and turn a workspace on or off while the room wash responds.

FORM: Code-led editorial product story superseding the earlier comp at the user's request. Apple/OpenAI restraint, real application evidence, functional glass chrome, softly graded mineral daylight, and colorful Hue state.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
